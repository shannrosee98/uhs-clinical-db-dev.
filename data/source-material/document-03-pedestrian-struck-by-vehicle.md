# OVERVIEW

A pedestrian struck by a vehicle may have multiple serious injuries even when there is little visible external damage. Consider the possibility of primary impact, secondary impact with the road or another object, and run-over or crush injury.

## Common RP concerns

- Head injury
- Neck or spinal injury
- Chest trauma
- Abdominal or pelvic injury
- Multiple fractures
- Open fractures
- Internal bleeding
- Crush injury
- Respiratory compromise
- Loss of consciousness
- Ejection or being thrown
- Vehicle run-over
- Significant road-surface injury
- Severe external bleeding

# 1 · COMMAND AND ROLE RULES

## Ambulance Team

- **Head of Ambulance:** Oversees ambulance operations and resource allocation.
- **Ambulance Team Leader:** Leads the ambulance response and assigns tasks.
- **Consultant Paramedic:** Provides senior ambulance clinical leadership.
- **Advanced Paramedic:** Manages serious and critical patients within server permissions.
- **Paramedic:** Performs assessment, treatment and transport within scope.
- **Student Paramedic:** Assists and observes under supervision.

## HART Team

- Manages high-risk scenes, technical rescue, vehicle hazards and entrapment.
- Coordinates with fire, police and ambulance command.
- Does not move the patient without an agreed rescue plan.

## HEMS Team

- Provides specialist support where authorised.
- Coordinates with the ground clinical lead.
- The pilot controls aviation safety and does not make clinical decisions.

## Hospital Team

- Receives and reassesses the patient.
- Provides emergency, ward, theatre and critical-care role-play.
- Consultant Doctors lead complex cases.
- Junior Doctors and Medical Students work under supervision.
- Hospital Dispatch coordinates patient reception, records and movement.

## General rules

1. Use `/me` for your character’s actions.

# 4 · OBSERVATIONS

## Stable pattern

- Heart rate: 78–100 bpm
- Respiratory rate: 12–20/min
- Oxygen saturation: 96–100% on air
- GCS: 15
- Pain: 1–5/10
- Warm skin and normal colour
- No significant mechanism-related red flags

## Concerning pattern

- Heart rate: 100–120 bpm
- Respiratory rate: 20–28/min
- Oxygen saturation: 92–95% or falling
- GCS: 13–15 or confused
- Pain: 6–9/10
- Pale, cool or sweaty skin
- High-energy impact or multiple injuries

## Critical pattern

- Heart rate: 120 bpm or higher, or abnormally slow with deterioration
- Respiratory rate: below 8, above 30 or visibly inadequate
- Falling blood pressure
- Oxygen saturation below 92% or rapidly falling
- GCS 8 or less or it is worsening. /me asks about chest pain, wheeze, cough, choking and previous respiratory problems.

- Gross limb deformity

- Suspected pelvic or abdominal injury
- Absent distal pulse
- Gross limb deformity
- Open fracture
- Suspected internal bleeding
- High-speed collision

- Unsafe scene
- Cardiac or respiratory arrest
